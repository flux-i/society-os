# Rules, private incident reports and supplied fines — expectation brief

Recorded 5 October 2026 before implementation, following [collections](collections-workflow.md). This preserves the next complete workflows from the [operations roadmap](society-operations-roadmap.md). These are implementation expectations; no incident/fine gate has passed yet. Development rules, allegations, pictures and amounts are fictional. Actual society rules, state, authority, notice/response periods and fine powers remain production acceptance inputs.

## Private reporting and independent review

An Administrator/Committee operator prepares a frozen rule version with title, supplied text, policy/authority reference, effective dates and whether supplied policy permits proposing a fine. A different current operator publishes it; the author cannot approve it. Withdrawal, retirement and replacement preserve each original version and review. No rule introduces an inferred fine amount or interest rate.

Currently related residents can report an incident: incident date, published applicable rule version, tagged flat, comment and an optional PNG/JPEG picture. The selector exposes at most 500 flat identifiers/labels, without occupants, phone numbers or former relationships. A resident must have a current society relationship to submit. The incident's tagged flat need not be the reporter's home. A picture is supporting evidence, not confirmation of misconduct. Prefer the device camera through a normal file input where supported; desktop upload remains available.

Preserve the private original with uploader, checksum, actual media validation and bounded dimensions/bytes. Never embed unchecked content or trust a file extension. Any permitted in-portal image preview is a newly encoded derivative without EXIF/location/device metadata; the original remains a separately authorised attachment. Authorised original/preview retrievals retain an access audit with current actor, opaque picture identity and the retrieved checksum, without filename, allegation or device metadata. This records authorised retrieval, not proof that the recipient saved the download. Preparing evidence does not publish it or create a debt. Unsupported/damaged images fail visibly with a useful replacement path.

The workflow is Reported → Needs information / Under review → Dismissed / Substantiated. Revisions retain the prior submitted version and decision. The reporter cannot substantiate their own case. Substantiation requires a current different operator, the expected version and a reason. Treasury permission alone grants no operational allegation decision. Changed rule dates, withdrawal, incorrect flat tags and duplicates require a deliberate review path, never a hidden rewrite. A reporter can withdraw an unresolved own report. Reopening or correcting a decision retains its earlier history and requires an explicit reason.

No public allegation feed exists. Reporters see only their own submission. Operational handlers see cases they can process. Subjects see only a deliberately issued, frozen response notice for their current home; they never receive reporter identity, private staff comments, unrelated cases or the original uploader's metadata. Sharing an allegation for response does not itself substantiate it. Responses are immutable submissions visible to authorised handlers and the responding person. Private changes never advance a subject's visible version/timestamp. Removal/current-home expiry immediately ends notice/evidence reads.

## Supplied fine proposals and finance posting

After the private incident workflow passes, deliver a separate complete fine checkpoint. A substantiated case and supplied fine permission allow a Treasury operator to propose a specific amount, accountable flat, policy/rule-version reference, reason, response/appeal handling and explicit due date. Review the frozen proposal before submission. A different current Treasury reviewer must authorise issuance. The reporter must not approve a fine arising from their own report. A disputed case cannot silently proceed: record the explicit policy decision and response status before issuance.

A substantiated incident alone creates zero charges. A pending fine creates zero charges. Issuance posts exactly one immutable CHARGE into the existing ledger with a stable fine identity, actor, rule version and original notice. It creates no received-money receipt. Never use a rule's optional amount as an automatic debit. Ended Treasury authority and missing factor verification deny both new issuance and accepted-operation replay.

Payment confirmation records money already received outside the portal through the shared original receipt contract and same-home explicit allocation. A claim or photo creates no receipt. Appeal, pause, dismissal after issuance, a supplied waiver and reversal retain links to the original charge, decision and receipt. A pause affects the actionable deadline/status according to the explicit supplied decision; it never invents a finance reversal or deletes history. Reversed sources release ineffective live allocations without automatically moving money to another purpose.

Independent financial example: a substantiated report and a pending supplied ₹250.25 fine leave debt and receipts unchanged. A different eligible issuance produces one ₹250.25 charge and zero receipts. Verifying and allocating an already-paid ₹100.00 produces one original receipt and ₹150.25 outstanding. Repeating issuance/payment confirmation preserves the same identities and amounts. A separately approved full waiver removes the live charge through a retained correction, releases the ₹100.00 allocation back to same-home credit and preserves the original receipt. Two people reporting the same payment must never produce two received entries.

## Private-report checkpoint contract, before code

The candidate is 0.13/schema 12; the accepted 0.12 preview stays pinned while it develops. Current operational officers may report as well as current residents, with the same self-review prohibition. Ended residents retain their own report's textual history but cannot submit, revise, respond or retrieve evidence. Treasury-only staff can use currently related resident participation when applicable; financial authority alone exposes no private allegations.

Rules are separately approved immutable proposals, with replacement versions linked to their published/retired original. Publication of a replacement deliberately retires its original, leaving old cases linked to the original text and dates. New reports require a currently published rule whose supplied effective interval includes the incident date, and reject future incidents. Retired rules remain readable in retained cases; they cannot receive new reports.

Pictures are at most 5 MiB, 4,096 pixels per axis and eight million decoded pixels. Decode actual PNG/JPEG bytes before reserving a writer; at most two simultaneous HTTP picture validations are permitted. Re-encode a preview of at most 1,280 pixels on its longest edge; no original metadata is copied. Immutable original and derivative checksums/bytes remain in private SQLite tables and restore with their reports. Existing document bytes and both picture forms count toward 100 MiB per-uploader and 1 GiB society quotas. An attachment belongs to one reporter/case; an unused picture can only be read by its uploader or a current handler. Subjects receive only the derivative explicitly frozen into their current notice. There is no automatic library publication.

Reporter-visible progress and history have their own version and timestamp. Private staff notes cannot change either. A reporter may revise an unresolved Reported/Needs-information submission. While a response notice is active, its home, rule, date and picture cannot be silently replaced; the handler must deliberately remove/reissue the notice for such a correction. Every subject response is immutable and belongs to the exact frozen notice and responding account. A subject sees only their own responses; an officer sees all responses. Reporter identity and staff activity remain absent from subject lists, searches, versions and timestamps.

The finite backend inventory will cover separate rule publication/replacement/retirement; current participation and cross-home selector privacy; exact picture bytes, derivative metadata, corruption, wrong types, limits, quota and immutable retry; reporter versus subject versus handler scope; separate decisions and zero financial effects; frozen notice/revision/removal and ended-home reads; private/public version separation; response ownership, operation replay, stale concurrent actions and current authority/factor expiry. Add strict HTTP Origin/CSRF/body/binary checks, populated schema-11 preservation and a nonempty original/preview/notice/response recovery case. Browser journeys must use real visible forms and captures; native execution must discover and execute bounded metadata reads in installed Chrome. These are planned checks, with no passes claimed yet.

The overview will add an independently retriable incident source before this checkpoint closes. It counts different-actor operational reviews, separately approvable rules, the reporter's own requested clarification and current-home notices still awaiting that person's response. Its bounded preview omits report text, evidence, identities and private notes; every row opens its exact permitted rule, case or notice. Responded/removed notices and ended participation leave the attention count. Source outages remain unknown and do not block other sections.

Prepared human decisions belong to one record and version. Paging unchanged history preserves the written reason and deliberate review; rereading a changed version keeps the reason but clears the selected action and review attestation. Following a linked original starts a fresh form for that original. Recovery feedback and its retry action must be fully visible inside the phone dialog. These existing review/pending expectations are made explicit by the rendered failure checks before acceptance.
