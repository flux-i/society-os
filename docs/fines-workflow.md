# Supplied fines — expectation brief before implementation

Recorded 6 October 2026 for the checkpoint after [private incidents](incidents-workflow.md), under the [operations roadmap](society-operations-roadmap.md). This is a plan, with no implementation or passes claimed. All local rules, notices, policies, allegations and amounts are fictional. Actual fine authority, liability, notice periods and appeal handling are society acceptance inputs.

## Outcome and authority

A substantiated incident can support a separately prepared, reviewed fine. Substantiation, a fine proposal, a response notice and a payment report create no financial entry or receipt. Only a deliberate eligible Treasury issuance posts one supplied charge. The portal never initiates payment or calculates a statutory penalty.

Current Treasury operators prepare and decide financial proposals. Registry administration and operational incident handling alone grant no financial posting power. Financial reviewers receive only the supplied substantiated outcome, tagged home, frozen rule and deliberately issued notice/response context needed for the financial decision; private allegations, reporter identity and original pictures remain under incident permissions. The server can enforce reporter conflicts without disclosing that identity to a financial reader.

A proposer cannot approve their own fine. A reporter cannot approve a fine arising from their own allegation, even if subsequently given Treasury authority. Approval, issuance, corrections and accepted-operation replay recheck current authority, confirmed factors, fresh authentication and expected versions inside the writer transaction. Current entitled residents see their own permitted financial records; a selected response notice has an explicit current-home audience rather than silently granting ledger access.

## Proposal, response and issuance

Preparation selects a substantiated case and supplied fine permission, freezes its relevant rule/home/date/outcome identity, and supplies an exact positive amount, policy/authority reference, reason, due date and notice/response handling. There is no inferred amount, liable person, interest rate or response period. Preview the exact charge, source and intended audience before submission.

Preserve the incident's deliberately issued frozen notice and its responses. If the fine amount or proposed decision needs a separate household notice, prepare and independently approve that specific wording/amount/audience before issuance; publishing it creates no debt. Current subjects can submit immutable private responses. Their own reply history must not expose other residents' responses, reporter identity, internal reviews or private versions. A handler's private note cannot advance a subject's visible notice version or timestamp.

An eligible different Treasury reviewer must deliberately record the supplied notice/response resolution and authorise issuance. New responses or a changed material source invalidate an old prepared resolution. An unresolved dispute cannot silently proceed; record the supplied resolution/policy decision. A case that is reopened, dismissed, retagged or otherwise materially changed cannot issue through a stale proposal. Private operational notes alone do not change the substantiated outcome identity.

Issuance produces exactly one immutable `CHARGE` through the shared ledger, with a stable fine/source identity. Concurrent attempts or a lost response preserve that charge identity. No received-money receipt is generated. Issued amounts and source references cannot be silently edited; linked corrections preserve the original. Decline/withdraw/replacement preserve unissued proposals and decisions.

## Already-paid money, dispute and correction

Use the shared original received-entry/receipt contract for money already paid externally. An entitled person may submit an already-paid claim; a different eligible Treasury reviewer verifies the supplied external source, or deliberately links compatible previously posted manual money. Reuse the global normalized external payment identity so two people reporting the same payment cannot create two received entries. A private picture remains a claim, not confirmation. Confirmation and retries must retain the original receipt.

Allocate only explicit usable same-home credit to a live charge. Combine maintenance allocations, fund attributions and fine allocations under the existing global cap. Partial and excess amounts, paid claims awaiting verification and confirmed allocations must have different labels. Linking a receipt or confirming a claim never automatically moves money from another purpose.

Record a supplied appeal/dispute and an explicit pause/resolution. Pausing changes the actionable deadline/status according to the supplied decision; it does not erase debt, reverse received money or imply a statutory result. Reopening an operational source after issuance flags the financial decision for review; it never deletes an issued charge automatically.

A proposed waiver or reversal needs its own source, reason, current expected version and different eligible reviewer. Preserve the original charge, decision, evidence links and receipt. Reversing/replacing a charge releases ineffective allocations back to the same home's available credit without automatically moving them elsewhere. A partial replacement must be deliberately reallocated. Correcting confirmed received money retains its original receipt and invalidates its ineffective allocation; it creates no fictional refund.

## Concrete local checkpoint contract, before code

The fine candidate will be 0.14/schema 13 after the 0.13 incident checkpoint is accepted. Hold its matching retained preview while developing fines. An incident has at most one active fine proposal/issued fine; declined or withdrawn preparations may be deliberately replaced with retained links. An issued fine changes through its own correction trail, never through a second unrelated debit for that same case.

Preparation is Pending. A different eligible Treasury reviewer can approve the frozen fine-specific household notice, moving it to Notified without creating a charge. Decline and author withdrawal preserve the preparation. Notified subjects see only that approved wording, supplied amount and dates for a currently related home. They may submit immutable responses even when their relationship grants no ledger access. This deliberate notice does not grant access to other financial records. Treasury officers see the notice's full response context; each resident sees only their own replies.

Issuance requires a deliberately supplied notice/response resolution, reviewed current responses and a current substantiated material source. Where the supplied response date has not passed, an explicit response or supplied policy decision must explain why issuance may proceed; there is no inferred statutory exception. Preserve that decision with the charge. A new response invalidates a prepared resolution. Current-case substantiation identity, tagged home, applicable frozen rule and deliberately issued response context determine the material source; an internal note alone cannot invalidate it. Reopening, dismissing or correcting the source blocks unissued stale proposals and flags issued fines for review without automatically deleting money.

A retired rule can remain the historical basis of an incident within its frozen effective dates where its supplied policy permits a fine. Retirement alone does not rewrite that rule or its old case. New reports still require a currently published applicable rule. No supplied fine permission means no fine proposal, even if a case is substantiated.

Use the existing shared `verified_fund_payments` identity register for both fund and fine confirmations despite its historical table name. Preserve its schema and prior rows; normalized external source/payment identity identifies one original received entry across purposes. A duplicate claim for the same fine links its already verified money without another receipt or automatic allocation. Any later movement of remaining credit is an explicit allocation decision. Claims against another home's money, mismatching original receipts and combined maintenance/fund/fine uses above available credit are denied transactionally.

An issued dispute is an immutable submission. A current eligible different Treasury reviewer records its supplied resolution and any explicit pause/date. A pause changes actionability and attention, preserving the supplied original due date, ledger balance, charge and receipt. A passed pause date requests review rather than silently deciding an appeal. Pausing does not prevent recording genuinely received money. Partial/full waivers require their own frozen proposal and separate approval; original and replacement entries and released credit remain visible.

Separate financial detail from current-home notice reads and private payment/appeal authorship. Actor-bound retries recheck the appropriate current role, factor and home before returning any previously accepted identity. Final acceptance includes those distinctions, material changes versus private notes, response races, cross-purpose duplicate money, partial/full corrections and exact amounts, with the same rendered/native/recovery gates as incidents.

Money reports, ledger details and receipt downloads retain the existing current financial-home entitlement. A current-home notice permits a response and an issued-fine appeal without granting those wider financial powers. Owners and tenants use the actual `can_view_finances` relationship supplied by the society; the software does not infer liability or financial access from the relationship label. Resident-facing fine reads expose the deliberately authorised notice and permitted issued financial effect, with private case identity, reporter, financial preparations/notes, other people's replies and private decision versions omitted. A notice keeps its frozen publication identity/time; private notes cannot masquerade as a household update.
